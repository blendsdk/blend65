# Roadmap: Conformance & Expressiveness

> **Feature-Set**: Conformance & Expressiveness

> **Created**: 2026-07-21
> **Last Updated**: 2026-07-22
> **Progress**: 0 / 14
> **Findings source**: [`codeops/00-conformance-triage.md`](../../00-conformance-triage.md)
>
> **Why a separate feature.** `asm-parity` measures output quality — generated bytes and cycles
> against hand-written twins, over programs that compile. This feature covers a different axis:
> whether the program can be written and whether it runs correctly. The parity scoreboard is
> structurally blind to everything here, which is why six green RDs passed over four silent
> miscompiles. `blend65-ri` is closed and this work corrects one of its closure claims, so it
> cannot live there either.
>
> **Governing bar**: the Prime Directive (project `CLAUDE.md`), with the two clauses added by
> G-03 below.

**Preflight iteration 2 (fresh session, 2026-07-22) ❌ BLOCKED — 6 critical + 6 major (PF-028…PF-049).**
21 of 27 iteration-1 findings verified fixed; all nine defects reproduce at HEAD. The revision widened
the defect *inventory* completely but not **AR-1**, the resolution everything leans on. Every critical
is downstream of it:

- **AR-1's carry-based exit is wrong on two axes and unbuildable at its seam.** Carry is the *unsigned*
  wrap flag — signed wrap is V (`sbyte to 127` hangs, interior signed loops that work today break).
  The word wrap is the high byte's carry. And it is **inexpressible**: `ILTerminator` has no flag
  branch, `translate.ts` forbids flags crossing a block boundary, and no `INC`/`DEC` emission site
  exists — so AR-1's stated shape cannot even emit headline defect M-01a. Iteration 1's PF-005 recurring

  sign handling free), retain the bound compare as a supplement.
- **The mandated idiom would regress correct code.** `0 to 253 step 2` and `-5 to 5` run correctly today;
  a carry-only exit overshoots / exits early. Interior and zero-trip loops miscompile under AC-4's
  "replace the compare" reading. The trigger is *not* "bound at the extreme" — `0 to 254 step 2` hangs
  with an interior bound.
- **R2/AC-3 vs AR-10/AC-12 are mutually unsatisfiable**: `slice8b`'s golden pins a runtime-bound loop
  (`0 to last`, `last = len-1`) that R2 forces to change and AC-12 forbids. Blast radius = exactly one
  golden; resolving it also discharges AC-13's idiom pin for free.
- **M-03 has a third population the RD misses**: sibling collision **truncates reads** of the wider
  declaration (`pokew($D000, t)` with `t:word` colliding a `t:byte` sibling emits a one-byte store).

### P3 — RD-02 Memory-access conformance

The single largest unblock: screen RAM, colour RAM, bitmaps, sprite-pointer tables.

- Implements `spec/12-intrinsics.md:159`, which **mandates** the runtime form
- Design bar: `poke(BASE + i, v)` must lower to absolute-indexed `STA base,X` (4 cyc);
  `(zp),Y` reserved for genuinely dynamic addresses. Uniform ZP-indirect would itself be a
  Prime Directive clause-1 defect
- ⚑ **The spec's letter says ZP-indirect.** That divergence needs an explicit AR **and** an
  errata-log entry at authoring, or a literal-minded implementation ships uniform `(zp),Y`
  "because the spec says so"
- ⚑ Depends on P1: acceptance fixtures want `for (i = 0 to 255)`, the canonical page loop
- Additive for the existing 14 goldens. ⚑ Keep the `boing-ball`/`balloon-color` unrolled-poke
  rewrites **out** of scope or budget the regen explicitly. New fixtures register in the
  coverage manifest

### P6 — RD-07 Constant folding & strength reduction — **batched with #70** ⚑

`x / 2` → `LSR` (currently `JSR __rt_div8`, ~150 cyc), `x % 8` → `AND`, `y * 40` → shifts,
array fill-init loop, `switch` jump table — **plus #70 local constant propagation**, which the
triage sequenced but the draft dropped ⚑.

Batched because both are "stop calling the runtime for constants" over the same corpus:
`slice5a`'s `v * 3` is RD-07's, `slice3b`'s `a * b` is #70's. Separately they regenerate the
corpus twice for two halves of one idea.

⚑ Also the natural home for the **G3 deadlock decision** — asm-parity RD-06 Rules 2–3 are
deferred over MMIO concerns while FUT-018 (the volatility discriminator that resolves them) is
deferred because no optimizer needs it. Break it here or give it a fresh, non-expiring rationale.

### P7 — RD-08 Diagnostics registry — **an instrument, not a sweep** ⚑

The draft placed a one-time sweep *before* RD-09/RD-10 mint new codes, contradicting its own
principle. Deliverable is instead a permanent **registry↔emitter agreement test** (every
registered code has a named emitter or a recorded waiver; every emitted code is registered),
plus the one-time reconciliation of the 17 dead codes, the missing registrations, and the
renumbering errata. With the instrument, position stops mattering and the drift class closes.

⚑ Note RD-01 already forces registry work early (M-03 needs `E10062`, currently unregistered).

### P8 — RD-09 Call-an-address ⛔ gated on D-02

`grill_me` first: a `call(addr)` intrinsic vs typed function pointers (FUT-003). Gates all
music — `F015`'s embedded-SID example rests on `callAddress()`, a helper the language cannot
express. Likely needs a `spec/` edit.

### P9 — RD-10 Platform-library mechanism

Module resolution, `--library`, zero-cost calls/inlining. **Library content explicitly out of
scope.** ⚑ Also where G-04's "parity pairs in library style" first becomes measurable, and a
potential corpus-wide regen — correctly last.

⚑ **U9 (cast syntax)** — spec documents `byte(x)` and `x as t`; the parser accepts only
`<byte>(x)`, so every cast example in the spec is a parse error. Homeless in the draft; assign
here or to RD-04 once D-02 rules.

## Lane interaction with `asm-parity`

These are **two lanes that interleave**, not one queue. Written down explicitly because leaving
it implicit is precisely how six deferrals were orphaned at RD-18's closure.

| Rule | |
|------|--|
| **L-1** | `asm-parity` pauses only for **P0-core → P1 → P2** (hours + one small RD). Not for P3–P9 |
| **L-2** | The pause is not because parity *measurements* are invalid — they are valid for what they measure, since no corpus fixture exercises the broken forms. It is because byte-tuning on a compiler that silently emits an infinite loop is the wrong use of attention |
| **L-3** | **#70 is not blocked — it is absorbed.** It is RD-07 here, batched with strength reduction, because `slice5a`'s `v * 3` and `slice3b`'s `a * b` are two halves of one idea and separately they regenerate the corpus twice |
| **L-4** | **No new parity pairs or twins until after P6.** A twin authored earlier goes stale at every later behavioural phase — the hazard asm-parity RD-16's own row records about RD-13. This is the only hard wait |
| **L-5** | **Re-scope, do not re-run**: asm-parity RD-12 (Sweep A, frontend conformance #57) is substantially discharged by this feature's audit, and RD-09 (Sweep D, lowering & instruction selection #60) overlaps RD-07. Both should be re-scoped against the triage rather than started fresh |
| **L-6** | Parallel-safe throughout: asm-parity RD-10 (#59), RD-08 (#54), RD-11 (#62), RD-14 (#63), T-01 (#55), T-03 (#73). RD-06's Rule 1 may proceed; its Rules 2–3 await the deadlock decision at P6 |

## Cross-cutting rules

⚑ **No new parity pairs until after P6.** RD-16 (`boing-ball` twin) is unblocked and #70 is
sequenced, but any twin authored mid-sequence goes stale at every later behavioural phase —
the hazard RD-16's own row already records.

⚑ **Every regenerating phase carries its ratchet re-derivation and scoreboard refresh in the
same commit.** The freshness gate hard-fails and rebuilds every pair from source; a plan whose
Verify command is weaker than CI has produced a CI-red-by-construction phase before. Bytes
ratchets must be re-derived on **shrink** too, or they go permanently slack.

⚑ **Probe freshness.** C-01's probes qualify leads for *task* eligibility. Each RD's preflight
must re-verify at HEAD before it earns a *plan* — an answer probed at P0 and consumed at P6,
after four phases of `lower.ts` churn, is stale on arrival.

**Per-phase oracle discipline.** This project's recurring failure is not an untrusted toolchain
but an **unfailable oracle** — a guard that is green before the implementation exists. Every new
assertion is perturbed once and watched to fail, then restored.
